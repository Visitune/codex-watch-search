Introduction People have the right to expect the food that they eat to be safe and suitable for consumption. Foodborne illness and foodborne injury can be severe or fatal or have a negative impact on human health over the longer term. Furthermore, outbreaks of foodborne illness can damage trade and tourism. Food spoilage is wasteful, costly, threatens food security and can adversely affect trade and consumer confidence. International food trade and the flow of travellers are increasing, bringing important social and economic benefits. However, this also makes the spread of illness around the world easier. Eating habits have undergone major changes in many countries and new food production, preparation, storage, and distribution techniques have developed to reflect this. Effective food hygiene practices, therefore, are vital to avoid the adverse human health and economic consequences of foodborne illness, foodborne injury, and food spoilage.
